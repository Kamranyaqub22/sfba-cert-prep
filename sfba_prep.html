Important Exam Tips
  • Every question has 3 answer options and requires exactly 1 correct answer — there are no multi-select questions on this exam.
  • The 72% passing score is higher than most other Salesforce certifications — do not underestimate it.
  • Questions are scenario-based. Ask yourself: "What would a BA do FIRST?" or "What is the BA's role here?"
  • Collaboration with Stakeholders (24%) is the highest-weighted topic — prioritise it in your prep.
  • You have roughly 1 min 35 sec per question. Flag difficult ones and return after completing easier questions.
  • The exam tests process and professional judgement, not technical Salesforce configuration knowledge.
  • When two answers look similar, the correct one usually involves the BA facilitating or documenting rather than deciding unilaterally.

Journey Mapping

A Journey Map is a visual representation of a customer's end-to-end experience across all touchpoints — capturing actions, thoughts, emotions, and pain points at each stage to provide an outside-in perspective.

Benefits
  • Outside-in customer perspective
  • Reveals pain points internal maps miss
  • Identifies moments that matter
  • Drives experience-led requirements

Discovery Methods

🫂 Embodying

BA immerses in the customer's role — experiencing the process first-hand to feel the friction and unmet needs directly.

👁 Shadowing

Observing users in their natural environment as they work. Reveals workarounds, undocumented steps, and real-world deviations from official process.

🗣 Interviewing

One-on-one or group conversations to elicit needs, pain points, and objectives directly. Best for complex, sensitive topics or key stakeholders.

Key Frameworks

Consequence Scanning

Structured technique to identify potential unintended consequences of a product or feature on people, communities, and the planet. Run before committing to a solution direction.

Application Lifecycle Management (ALM)

Governs Salesforce changes across environments: Dev → QA → UAT → Production. The BA ensures requirements are reflected at each stage before changes reach production.

Center of Excellence (CoE)

Centralised governance for the Salesforce platform. Core benefits: Prioritise Across Teams · Streamline Processes · Plan for Growth · Reduce Risk

Types of BA Analysis

Enterprise: Organisation structure & context.
Strategy: Why change is needed.
Stakeholder: Who is involved & impacted.

Stakeholder Engagement Methods

  • Make It a Conversation — two-way dialogue, not a status broadcast
  • Share How You Can Help — frame the BA role as enabling the stakeholder's success
  • Get Commitment for Next Steps — end every interaction with a clear agreed action
  • Develop Relationships — trust is built over time through consistent follow-through

Study Notes — Other Exam Topics

Collaboration with Stakeholders (24% — ~14 Qs)

  • Choose the right elicitation technique: Interviews (complex/sensitive), Workshops (consensus), Surveys (large groups), Observation (undocumented processes), Prototyping (unclear UI needs)
  • RACI: Responsible · Accountable (only ONE per task) · Consulted · Informed
  • Communication plan: who gets what information, how often, in what format
  • Conflict resolution: surface, document, escalate to sponsor — the BA facilitates, not decides
  • Change champions: identify internal advocates to drive adoption across the organisation
Surveys = large/dispersed groups. Interviews = deep insight. Observation = reveals what people actually do. One size does not fit all.

Business Process Mapping (16% — ~10 Qs)

  • Order: As-Is map → Gap Analysis → To-Be map
  • Swimlane diagrams: lanes by role/department; show WHO does each step and handoffs between parties
  • Decision points: shown as diamonds; Yes/No branching gates
  • Exception paths: what happens when normal flow breaks — must be documented
  • Process elements: Trigger → Activities → Decision Points → Outcomes
Always complete the as-is map before designing to-be. Gap analysis happens after both maps exist.

Requirements (17% — ~10 Qs)

  • Types: Business · Stakeholder · Functional · Non-Functional · Technical · Transition
  • MoSCoW: Must Have / Should Have / Could Have / Won't Have (this time)
  • RTM: links requirements to their source and to test cases
  • Validation: confirming requirements represent the real need before building
  • Sign-off: formal stakeholder approval before development begins
"System must load in <2 seconds" = non-functional (performance). Won't Have ≠ never — it means out of scope for this release.

User Stories (18% — ~11 Qs)

As a [role], I want [capability], so that [benefit].
  • INVEST: Independent · Negotiable · Valuable · Estimable · Small · Testable
  • Acceptance Criteria (Given-When-Then): Given [context], When [action], Then [outcome]
  • Epic: a story too large for one sprint — must be split using SPIDR (Spikes, Paths, Interfaces, Data, Rules)
  • Definition of Done (DoD): applies to ALL stories. Acceptance Criteria = per story.
The "so that" clause is not optional — it captures business value and is the most important part of a user story.

User Acceptance Testing (8% — ~5 Qs)

  • UAT is performed by business users — not the BA or development team (BA facilitates and supports)
  • Happens after system testing, before go-live
  • Entry criteria: system testing passed, test data ready, participants confirmed
  • Exit criteria: all critical defects resolved, sign-off received
  • Defect severity: Critical (blocks go-live) → High → Medium → Low
  • Smoke test: quick sanity check that the build is stable enough to begin UAT — not a full test
  • Go/No-Go decision: facilitated by BA, decided by the business sponsor
High-severity defects may be deferred with stakeholder approval. Critical defects must be resolved before go-live.
